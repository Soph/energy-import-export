### 1. The EEG negative-price claim — **FACTUALLY WRONG**

&gt; L314–316: *"Only the market-driven half is visible here. When the price is below zero the support premium is withdrawn, so generating costs the operator money and the blades are feathered"*
&gt; (repeated verbatim in `build_data.py:280` and again at `:327`)

Stated as a general rule for German wind, this is wrong for the majority of the fleet in 2026. § 51 EEG is vintage- and size-graded:

| Commissioned | Trigger | Exempt below |
|---|---|---|
| before 1 Jan 2016 | **never** — no reduction at all | — |
| 2016–2020 | 6 *consecutive* hours | wind &lt; 3 MW |
| 2021–2022 | 4 consecutive hours | 500 kW |
| 2023 – 24 Feb 2025 | 4→3→2→1 h step-down | 400 kW |

Three problems. (a) The defining property of uniform pricing is that sellers clear at the *market* price, not at their own bid — infra-marginal sellers receive more than they bid. ### 9. Switzerland is not in the coupled day-ahead market — **FACTUALLY WRONG** for one of the eleven borders

&gt; L383–388: *"when a border is full, the two sides clear at different prices… the gap on each megawatt-hour that crossed is collected by the coupling mechanism and paid to the two transmission operators on that border"*

DE-CH day-ahead capacity is allocated **explicitly** via JAO auctions; Switzerland is outside SDAC. ### 10. "When a border is full, the two sides clear at different prices" — **MISLEADING BUT DEFENSIBLE**

True under NTC/ATC coupling. Under **flow-based** market coupling, which governs most DE borders (Core CCR: AT, BE, CZ, FR, NL, PL), price separation corresponds to a binding *critical network element*, which can be an internal line in a third country, with no DE border at its physical limit. Relatedly, the actual congestion income in Core is pooled at CCR level and then *distributed* to bidding-zone borders using |commercial flow × market spread| as the key, before the 50/50 split between the two TSOs. So your per-border `net flow × spread` is a good approximation *of the distribution key*, not of income collected on that border — and it uses net rather than absolute flow. The **50/50 split claim is FINE**. Sources: [ACER CID methodology](https://eepublicdownloads.entsoe.eu/clean-documents/nc-tasks/ACER_Decision_06-2023_on_CID_Annex_I.pdf), [CID explanatory note](https://www.acer.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/en/Electricity/MARKET-CODES/CAPACITY-ALLOCATION-AND-CONGESTION-MANAGEMENT/12%20CIDM/Action%203b%20-%20CIDM%20explanatory%20document.PDF).
