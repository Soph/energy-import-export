## What this is, and the verdict

A dependency-free static dashboard over a Python pipeline that values Germany's cross-border day-ahead flows per border and MTU, built on SMARD because it's the only source that is both CC-BY-licensed and gross-per-direction. The craft level is unusually high: the licensing analysis drives the architecture, the wind panel is built around an identification test rather than a headline number, sign-clipping bias in the old curtailment metric was found and documented, writes are atomic, the page is one file with hand-rolled SVG and a real dark mode. The main gaps are (a) one known data trap that the next cron run will actually spring, (b) a handful of small front-end bugs, and (c) a story that has outgrown its title — the strongest finding in the repo (the seasons disagree) isn't on the page at all.

## Technical findings

**1. The daily cron will quietly ruin the example-day panel.** `write_adjacency` guards against a narrow run overwriting a wide record, and its docstring names "the same trap `example_day` still has" — but it's still unfixed. The current `example_day.json` says "widest intraday price spread **on record**: 672 EUR/MWh" (2026-05-01), which the current code can no longer produce: it writes "widest … **in the refreshed range**", and the cron's range is the last 3 days. The first scheduled run replaces the most dramatic day of the year with whatever Tuesday was. Fix is the same guard: keep the file unless the new spread beats the recorded one (or recompute the spread from the month files already on disk, which needs no extra fetch).

**2. Sticky tooltip** (verified in the browser): hover any chart, scroll — the `position: fixed` tooltip stays at full opacity floating over unrelated panels until the pointer happens to leave a hit rect. On touch it's worse: a tap fires `pointerenter` and nothing ever dismisses it. One `scroll` listener calling `hideTip()` (and maybe a `pointerdown` outside) fixes both.

**3. Leftover dead code in `render()`** — `index.html:1557` computes `ownPx` and never uses it; `index.html:1569-1577` sets `volHint` via a ternary whose else-branch is immediately repeated in `if (!hasT)`, next to a `const _unused = netted;`. Clearly debris from the label-fix commit; worth sweeping before it confuses a future edit.

**4. Resolution: the analysis is hourly in a 15-minute market.** `align()` resamples everything to the `--freq` grid (default 60min) by mean, and the comment acknowledges day-ahead moved to 15-minute MTUs. For the balance and rent this is a small real error, not just smoothing — net position and price both vary within the hour, and the product of means isn't the mean of products. For the wind panel it's material: "hours priced below zero" misses quarter-hours below zero inside a positive-mean hour, and the panel's own § 51 EEG text explains that post-Feb-2025 plants respond to the *first negative quarter-hour* — a mechanism the hourly analysis literally cannot see. Running `build_data.py` at `--freq 15min` costs nothing on the published side (aggregation to daily happens in the pipeline) and would make the negative-hour counting match the market it describes.

**5. Small ones:** a failed month fetch inside a range-button click is an unhandled rejection — the button silently does nothing (worth an error surface). The month files ship six daily wind fields (`wind_fc_neg_gwh` etc.) the page never reads — fine if intentional as an audit trail, dead payload otherwise. The hero's `paid/earned` ratio would print nonsense if `earned` ever goes negative (possible in a deeply negative-price window).

## Presentation

What works: leading with the €127/MWh gap instead of the balance was the right reframe; the "three facts" card defuses the bad-faith reading before it starts; the red/blue money language is consistent everywhere including tooltips; transit as the neutral pole is a genuinely good encoding decision; the wind chart showing the *adjacency test* rather than a volume is the best panel on the page — the shape is the argument, as the caption says. Labels state their basis, the extreme bar is the only one labeled, and the table view backs everything.

What's weaker:

- **The headline claim is under-evidenced on screen.** The subtitle says the price "swings several-fold within a day," but the only intraday evidence is one fixed example day. Everything else is daily aggregates. An average day-profile for the selected window (price by hour-of-day, with the importing/exporting hours shaded red/blue) would directly connect the hero to the charts and prove the claim for *this* window, not one hand-of-god day.
- **"By border" is the orphan panel.** It's the only card with no takeaway sentence, the zone codes (DK1/NO2/SE4) are unexplained for anyone who isn't already in the field, and balance-only bars hide the difference between a quiet border and one with huge two-way churn that nets out. Even a hint line ("France is the biggest one-way supplier; the Netherlands mostly a corridor") would lift it to the standard of the rest.
- **The wind card is the text-heaviest thing on the page** — three dense paragraphs, one of which is a § 51 EEG commissioning-date taxonomy. The congestion card already shows the right pattern: headline + `<details>` for the law. Fold the who-stopped paragraph the same way.
- **The levels panel invites a question it doesn't answer**: the dynamic household tariff (€385) sits *above* the fixed new contract (€365), on a page arguing that timing is where the value is. There's presumably a good reason (fixed two-thirds plus an expensive spot month), but the reader who's followed your argument will trip exactly there — one clause in the hint would catch them.
- Cosmetic: the balance axis prints "0.0M"; "Days arrivals worth more: 25 of 30" takes two reads to parse.

## The story

The honest observation first: **the page has outgrown its title.** "Germany's cross-border power balance" describes the original script. The dashboard's actual subject — visible in what the hero says, what the wind and example-day and who-pays panels do — is *what an hour of electricity is worth in Germany, and who feels it*. Trade is the evidence, not the subject. I'd either retitle in that direction ("The value of an hour of German power") or accept the drift consciously.

The arc I'd propose, mostly by reordering what exists:

1. **One market, two prices.** Hero (€127 apart, 4.1×), then immediately the example day — the mechanism explainer currently arrives sixth, after three panels that presuppose it. Show *how* a price happens before showing prices happening.
2. **Trade follows the clock.** Price-when-importing/exporting, daily balance, volumes with the transit share ("Germany as a corridor"), by border. This is the original story, intact.
3. **Supply answers the price.** Residual load, then wind-goes-missing-below-zero. Right now the wind panel interrupts the trade arc; as act three it becomes the payoff — the market doesn't just move power across borders, it switches generators off.
4. **Who ends up paying.** Congestion ledger into who-actually-pays-what — both already end at the same place, the grid fee on a bill.

And the **missing chapter is sitting in your backfill commit**: January and July are different systems. The price gap is largely a summer effect (€72 vs €120), Germany flips from net exporter to net importer, solar's cannibalisation *vanishes* in winter (+1.2 premium vs −50.5 discount), and congestion rent nearly doubles in January. That's the strongest finding the project has produced and it's currently invisible unless someone presses "All" and squints. A monthly-strip panel — gap, net position, solar market value by month — would carry it, and it also inoculates the page against its own 30-day default window telling a materially different story depending on when it's read (which the wind panel already had to defend against once).
